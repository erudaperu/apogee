# Mechanical design and CAD

I want to design and build my own model rocket, including an airframe and avionics assembly that I can explain, inspect and service. I know CAD, so I will use that familiarity while learning the rocketry-specific principles and review process. My physical design and electronics packaging will develop together from the first concept.

I will use a certified commercial motor and keep motor development outside my design work. Before committing to a configuration, I will learn established model-rocketry practice and seek local club or experienced supervision appropriate to my first build.

## Planned deliverables

| Deliverable | What I need it to show |
| --- | --- |
| CAD assembly | Component positions, mounting and access |
| Physical concept | Airframe, instrumentation space and recovery approach, with assumptions and review questions |
| Dimensioned drawings | Critical fit dimensions and the assumptions behind them |
| Mass-property record | Estimated values separated from measured values |
| Assembly notes | Order of assembly, connectors and service access |
| Fit-check record | Photos, measured interference and revisions |
| Parts list | Sourced items, custom parts, quantity and actual cost |

## Questions I will resolve first

- Which CAD tool can I use and version reliably?
- Which parts suit the 3D printers I can access, once their capabilities and materials are confirmed?
- How will I remove the electronics without damaging wiring or mounts?
- How will I keep sensor orientation consistent and documented?
- Which clearances need a physical fit check rather than a visual CAD check?

## First acceptance check

I will produce a packaging concept tied to the chosen electronics dimensions, export a readable drawing, and document a physical fit check before describing it as a built assembly. A render alone will count as a design artifact, not proof of fit or flight suitability.

**Current status:** planning. I have CAD familiarity and access to 3D printers, but no CAD model for this rocket yet. The software tool, printer capabilities, materials, dimensions and part-by-part manufacturing choices are still open.
